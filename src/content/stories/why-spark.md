Excellent research does not translate itself. A technically impressive model can still fail to create public value when it is disconnected from the people who will use it, the institutions that must support it, or the constraints that shape real decisions.

SPARK begins from a different premise: research translation is part of the research lifecycle, not a final handoff after publication.

That means asking practical questions early. Who experiences the problem? Who defines success? What evidence is needed before a tool can inform a decision? How should uncertainty be communicated? Which incentives make collaboration sustainable—and which ones pull partners apart?

## Connecting responsibility and implementation

Responsible AI cannot be reduced to a checklist added at the end of a project. Ethics, alignment, participation, accountability, and data governance influence how a scientific problem is framed, what is measured, whose knowledge is represented, and whether a result is appropriate for deployment.

SPARK therefore connects high-level reflection with technical practice. Participants will be invited to explore emerging AI methods, strengthen reproducible research skills, and work through realistic collaborative projects with attention to stakeholder needs and implementation pathways.

## More than one event

The forum is intended to seed relationships and reusable outputs that continue afterward. This hub will grow to host selected tutorials, project artifacts, community stories, events, and opportunities after appropriate review, consent, licensing, and accessibility checks.
